# Blog posts

A post is an announcement from the people who made the thing. It says "we made X
to do Y!" and invites the reader to try it. The reader is a person who likes
weird video, and the post should sound like a friend showing off something new.

This overrides the neutral register in `docs/WRITING.md`, which is for docs and
comments. Posts are not reference pages.

## Voice

- Open with what we made and what it does for you. Name the feature and the
  payoff in the first sentence, for example "We made the keyer cut out
  transparent images, so you can put a fox in a misty forest without a green
  screen!"
- Write "we" for the makers and "you" for the reader. The software is never the
  subject of a paragraph.
- Use active verbs and ordinary words. "The keyer fills the hole with its own
  earlier output" beats "The fill is sourced from the prior output".
- Show enthusiasm with specifics and the occasional exclamation mark. Say what
  looks good about the result, such as "it looks gloriously broken".
- Put the link to try it near the top, then explain.
- Explain the mechanism after the payoff, in one or two sentences, and say what
  it does to the picture. Keep every number, patent, source and control name,
  because the real mechanism is part of the fun.
- Explain a jargon term in a few words the first time, such as "the burst gate
  samples the wrong part of each line".

## Do not write

- Neutral spec sentences, such as "Key source switches the keyer's key input
  from the chroma slice of B to B's alpha channel". Say what you can now do.
- Hype adjectives such as `groundbreaking`, `stunning` and `powerful`, and
  claims we have not made, such as "our best yet". The checklist in
  `docs/WRITING.md` still applies to these.
- Section openers that name the program as the actor, such as "It runs" or "The
  preset models".
- A feeling we do not have. Add "we love" only where it is true.

## Keep

- Figures, captions, alt text, look links and licence credits stay as they are.
- Headings name the subject, in plain words.
- Dated updates stay at the bottom, and each says what we changed in the same
  voice.
