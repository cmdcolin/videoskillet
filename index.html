Every fault is a consequence of the signal, not an effect drawn on the picture.

This browser has no WebGPU. The signal path is compute shaders with no fallback renderer, so the app will open on a screen saying so — what runs it.

The picture fills the window. The panel holds the whole board — the look, the signal path, and the stage you have open on it.

Chroma gain is the colour control on the set: how hard the demodulated chroma is amplified before the picture is drawn. Past 1 the saturation blooms and clips against the edge of the gamut — and it is the decoder doing that, not a filter over the photograph.

The map is the rig: two sources into a mixer, then the channel it is recorded and broadcast over, the receiver that decodes it and the screen it lands on. Click a stage and its controls open under it.

How it works

A frame of NTSC is 525 lines of 910 voltage samples. That array lives on the GPU, and a chain of compute shaders rewrites it sixty times a second: encode the picture into the waveform, damage it, decode it back.

The faults act on the waveform. A loose cable, a bad ground or a slipping vertical hold change the voltages, and the dot crawl, tearing and hue shifts come out of the decoder on their own.

The screen can be fed back into the mixer, or looked at by a camera, so feedback runs through the whole chain too.
